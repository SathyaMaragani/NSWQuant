The algorithm: X, imbalance market maker with a cross-venue signal

1 · SIGNAL

Binance resting size within ±2.5 % of the price, bid vs ask: imb = (bid − ask) / (bid + ask), z-scored over the last hour.

2 · PRICE

fair = mid + c1·z − skew·position — lean towards where the deeper book points, away from inventory.

3 · QUOTE

Post-only bid = fair − half, ask = fair + half, never better than the best price. Re-quote once a second.

4 · RISK

Stop quoting a side at 20 lots of inventory. Queue position, latency, partial fills and fees simulated on every trade print.

Origin: the order-book-imbalance market maker from the open-source hftbacktest tutorials (MIT), ported to our simulator. Fed by Bybit's own book it loses (O below); fed by Binance's deeper ±2.5 % book, as in the original, it is X. The TinyML model (accuracy and speed above) powers NSQ-MM C and Q and the live paper trader.

Read this before quoting the numbers

  • All P&L is simulated: real recorded market data, simulated orders. No real money was traded.
  • The HFT-firm profile assumes co-location (10 µs) and a −0.5 bp maker rebate; on retail infrastructure every market maker here loses.
  • X was chosen as the best of several strategy families (A, C, Q, O, X, T), which flatters it; the multi-day test with frozen settings is the fairer measure.
  • Over 9 out-of-sample days, X on ETH is profitable after the rebate but slightly negative before it, and t = 1.83 is not statistically significant at 95 %. On BTC it has no edge. The robust finding is the signal itself: it beats the same quotes without it on 8 of 9 days in both markets.
  • The single-day figure for 10 Nov (+201.57 ETH) is one of the better days; quote the multi-day total, not the best day.
  • X earns partly by holding inventory in the signal's direction for minutes, so it carries directional risk; see the max drawdown column.
